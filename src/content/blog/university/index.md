After five long, tedious years, I've finally finished off my Bachelors degree in Software Engineering.
As nice as it is to be permanently rid of homework, my joy barely lasted a few days.

A dark thought started to bubble up in the back of my head over the years. I hadn't really
let myself think it properly before I was done and dusted, as a way to help myself power through.
However, now that I was free from my eternal obligation, it brought itself out into the light, weeping
and ugly.

> What was any of that for?

To what end did I spend all that time and energy on my course? What did I gain? And, most importantly,
where did all that money go?


---

## The cost
Let's set a foundation to focus on as we explore.

For some context -- I attended an average university in Australia, so my thoughts and feelings are
inevitably tinged by prolonged exposure to that pervasive "lucky country"[^1] miasma that hovers around a
great many of our institutions. Wherever you are may have it better, or may have it worse. I believe
most of what I want to say will still apply. Though it's somewhat cynical, I am deliberately using a
monetary framing, for reasons that will become obvious later.

As a holder of a Commonwealth supported place (CSP), the government helpfully subsidised
the cost of my tertiary education (partially out of my own taxes, of course), excluding an out-of-pocket
fee that adds up to somewhere in the ballpark of $40,000 AUD over the course of my degree. Including
the cost of the subsidy, the break-even price of my degree is supposedly within the neighborhood of
$25-30k AUD per year.
